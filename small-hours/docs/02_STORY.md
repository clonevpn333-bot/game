# 02 — STORY  ⭐ (SECOND MOST IMPORTANT)
### *The Ashgrove Study*

> **Spoiler warning:** this document contains the full plot and ending.

---

## Theme
Desperation makes you sign things you don't read. The horror is not that a
monster wants to hurt you — it's that an *institution* has decided your fear is a
resource, and you were poor enough to volunteer. It's about being **watched at
your most defenseless** and being too broke to leave.

The supernatural is kept ambiguous on purpose (the *Fears to Fathom* discipline):
maybe the Visitor is the drug; maybe the clinic *summons* what it sells; maybe
both. The game never tells you. The human evil is concrete. The rest is the cold
spot you can't explain on the drive home.

---

## Cast

- **ELI MERCER, 20 (you).** Community-college dropout-in-progress, two jobs short
  of rent, bank balance **-$43.18**. Sister Maya texts; Mom doesn't know how bad
  it is. Tired, decent, out of options. Bitten nails. You never see his face —
  *you are him* — except in reflections (which matters; see the Surprise).
- **MARCUS VANE, the Night Technician.** Friendly, patient, calls you "champ." The
  only staff member overnight. Knows the protocol cold because he's run it a
  hundred times. The warmth is real-seeming for exactly long enough. (Design:
  Art Bible §4.2.)
- **RENATA ORTIZ, Intake Coordinator.** Genuinely kind, overworked, leaves at
  11pm. Your last normal human. "You're in good hands with Marcus."
- **MAYA (offscreen, texts only).** Eli's younger sister. The reason the money
  matters. The voice that, late in the night, you desperately want to answer —
  and the phone that, late in the night, starts answering *itself.*
- **THE LONG SUBJECT (Room 4).** Someone who came for one night and never checked
  out. Proof of what this is.
- **THE VISITOR.** The thing at the foot of the bed. (Art Bible §5.) Unexplained
  by design.

---

## The truth (what's actually going on)

Ashgrove is not a sleep lab. It is a small, profitable operation that **records
genuine human terror** and sells it. The "study" is the delivery mechanism:

- The **consent forms** sign away your "session data and derivative recordings in
  perpetuity." Nobody reads page 4.
- The **conductive gel** and the **IV "hydration"** carry a compound that lowers
  the threshold between waking and REM and makes the hypnagogic state suggestible
  and *vivid* — it manufactures sleep paralysis and lucid nightmares on schedule.
- The **polysomnograph** isn't measuring health. It's measuring fear with clinical
  precision — and Vane, on the other side of the glass, **steers** the night:
  nudges the lights, plays sounds, opens a door, lets the Visitor in. He is a
  *director.* The subject is the performance. The tape is the product.
- The buyer is never named. (A logo on a shipping crate in the basement. A
  client list you glimpse but can't read. *Fears to Fathom* ambiguity: the worst
  answer is the one you supply yourself.)
- **The Visitor** is the part even Vane doesn't fully control. The clinic has done
  this so many times, in this building, that *something now comes on its own* when
  the fear gets high enough. Vane has stopped asking what it is. He just keeps the
  camera rolling, because **that footage sells best.**

The cruelty engine: the more afraid you are, the more valuable the night, so every
"safety" feature is a lie designed to keep you in the bed and producing.

---

## Structure (≈60 minutes, six beats)

The night is organized as **sleep cycles**, alternating *awake-in-the-clinic* and
*under* (dream/paralysis), so the player can never fully trust their footing.
Full minute-by-minute timing is in [`06_PACING_THE_HOUR.md`](06_PACING_THE_HOUR.md).

### Beat 1 — Arrival (≈0:00–0:08) — *the most normal you'll feel all night*
Drive to a half-empty strip mall, 10:40pm. Headlights, a buzzing sign:
**ASHGROVE SLEEP CENTER.** Your phone: the ad screenshot ("$1,200 / one night"),
a text from Maya ("did you get it??"), the bank app (-$43.18). Inside: Renata,
forms, a fish tank, a silent infomercial. You sign (the player physically signs —
and *can* read page 4 if they bother; almost nobody does, and the game quietly
notes whether you did). Renata gives you the gown. "Marcus'll get you settled.
You're in good hands." 11:00pm — she leaves. The door locks behind her with a
sound you'll remember.

### Beat 2 — Wiring up (≈0:08–0:18) — *trust, established to be betrayed*
Vane is warmth itself. Small talk that's actually data-gathering ("Any history of
nightmares? Ever felt like someone was in the room when you woke up?"). He wires
you to the harness — gel, cap, chest belt, pulse-ox, the IV "to keep you
hydrated." The rules, delivered kindly:
1. **Stay in the bed.** 2. **Don't remove the sensors** ("it ruins the night's
data and we can't pay out an incomplete session" — the money is the leash).
3. **Press the call button if you need anything; I'm right here watching your
readings.** He points at the camera's red dot, the one-way mirror. "Just sleep
naturally. Easy money." Lights down. The door clicks. You are alone, watched, and
told to do the one thing that requires not being watched.

### Beat 3 — First sleep (≈0:18–0:30) — *the fuse*
You **breathe yourself to sleep** (core mechanic). First cycle is almost gentle —
a dream of home, of Maya, warm and wrong at the edges. You wake (wet inhale, cold
blue). The clock has jumped. Small things are off: the call button is on the
floor now; the bathroom door is open; the EEG monitor through the glass shows a
spike that wasn't your heart. A text from Maya: *"you up?"* — but it's 3am and the
send time reads **3:33**, and you didn't hear the buzz. The filter grain ticks up.

### Beat 4 — The night turns (≈0:30–0:42) — *the rules start to cost*
Sleep paralysis (first full **eyes-only** sequence): you wake unable to move,
and the **Visitor is at the foot of the bed.** You can only move your eyes
(camera). Endure it. It's gone when you can move — but the room *isn't the same
room.* You explore the small space you're allowed; you find the call button does
nothing; the mirror, up close, shows a shape behind the glass that isn't you.
Vane's voice on the room speaker is still kind but answers things you didn't ask.
You realize the texts from Maya are coming from *inside the session* — the phone
on the nightstand lights with messages you're "receiving" while it sits untouched.
The temptation to **rip off the sensors and run** becomes overwhelming — and the
game makes you *feel* the wires as a leash (Gameplay §). The first time you try a
door: locked. From the *outside.*

### Beat 5 — Behind the glass (≈0:42–0:54) — *the reveal*
You get out (a vent, a propped fire door, a moment when Vane leaves his post — the
exact route is the player's small triumph). You cross into **the observation
room** and see it from his side: the monitors, the IR feed of *your own empty
bed*, the clipboard with your fear scored minute by minute, and the **wall of
tapes** — names, dates, Room 4's subject among them, and one tape that shouldn't
exist yet: tonight's, already labeled, already *partly recorded.* The basement
door. The truth, assembled by the player from props, not exposition. And then —
Vane, behind you, still smiling, not surprised at all. "You weren't supposed to be
up yet. But honestly? This is even better." Because **you being awake and running
is the best footage of all.** The chase is not a chase; it's a *shoot.*

### Beat 6 — Morning / the bill comes due (≈0:54–1:00) — *the gut punch*

Three endings, branched by the player's behavior across the night (see Gameplay &
Surprise — the branch is driven by the **Fear Profile**, not a menu choice):

- **"DISCHARGED" (you get out).** You make it to the parking lot at dawn. Your car.
  Your phone buzzes — a bank notification: **+$1,200.00, ASHGROVE WELLNESS LLC.**
  They paid you. Of course they paid you; you *delivered.* You drive home. The
  last shot: your rear-view mirror, the clinic shrinking — and at the foot of your
  back seat, the way the Visitor stood at the foot of the bed, *something is still
  with you.* Cut to the end-card (the Fear Profile). The money cleared. You'll
  need it next month, too. The ad is still up.
- **"INCOMPLETE SESSION" (you break and they sedate you).** If you panic past the
  threshold and tear everything off too early, Vane is there with a kindness that
  is the worst thing in the game. "Hey, hey — you're okay. Let's get you settled."
  Fade. You wake in your own bed days later, $1,200 richer, missing a night you
  can't account for, a fresh adhesive-rash on your chest, and Room 4's incurious
  patience starting to settle into you. The implication: you're a returning
  customer now.
- **"RETAINED" (Room 4).** If you do nothing — comply perfectly, never resist,
  let them have the whole night — you sleep through to a morning that isn't one.
  You're the long subject now. Someone new is checking in down the hall. You hear
  Renata's voice: "You're in good hands with Marcus."

All three roll into the **Fear Profile end-card** (the Surprise). The final line,
in all branches: *"Thank you for your contribution. We've learned so much about
you. We'll see you again soon."*

## Scene-by-scene script highlights (selected lines)

**Renata, checking you in:**
> "Big night! It's really nothing — you sleep, we watch the squiggles, you wake up
> twelve hundred bucks richer. People do it twice." *(beat)* "Page four's just the
> legal stuff, sign at the X."

**Vane, wiring you up (warm):**
> "This gel's a little cold, sorry — that's normal. You'll forget it's there in
> ten minutes." *(applying the cap)* "So — you ever wake up and feel like someone's
> standing right where I am now? No? ... You will tonight, probably. It's the
> study. Totally normal. Don't let it spook you."

**Vane, on the room speaker at 3am, kind:**
> "Heart rate's climbing a little, champ. That's *great*, actually. You're doing
> really well. Just stay in the bed for me."

**Vane, behind the glass, the turn:**
> "You weren't supposed to be up yet." *(genuinely delighted)* "But honestly? This
> is even better. Don't stop on my account."

**The end-card, last line (all endings):**
> "Thank you for your contribution. We've learned so much about you.
>  We'll see you again soon."

## The phone thread (the *Fears to Fathom* texting charm)
Eli's phone is the emotional anchor and a horror instrument:
- Early: real, mundane (Maya: "did you GET it??" / Mom: "drive safe baby" /
  landlord: "rent was due the 1st").
- Middle: the phone receives messages **while you're holding it and didn't feel
  it buzz**; timestamps that are wrong; Maya texting things Maya wouldn't.
- Late: the battery, your only light, dying; a call from your own number; the
  realization that the "Maya" you've been comforted by for an hour stopped being
  Maya around 3am, and you didn't notice — because you needed her to be real.

That last one — *you wanted the comfort so badly you didn't check the
timestamps* — is the quietest, meanest beat in the game, and it's pure *Fears to
Fathom.*
