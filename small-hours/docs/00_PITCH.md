# 00 — THE PITCH

**SMALL HOURS — Episode One: *The Ashgrove Study***
First-person psychological horror • single-player • ~60 minutes • no combat.

---

## The one line
You take a paid overnight sleep study to make rent. The people watching you
through the glass are not studying your sleep — they are studying your fear, and
by morning they will know you better than you know yourself.

## The hook (why this is *Fears to Fathom* and why it's new)
*Fears to Fathom* takes an ordinary, deeply relatable situation — a night shift,
home alone, a road trip — and lets dread leak into it until the ordinary becomes
unsurvivable. **Small Hours keeps that exact charm** (broke young protagonist,
real phone, real chores, mundane realism, a slow fuse, a cruel last shot) and
sets it somewhere no horror game has properly used: **a sleep clinic, where the
core verb of the game is the most defenseless thing a person can do — fall
asleep while strangers watch.**

Everyone has lain awake in a strange bed, certain something was in the room. This
game is built entirely out of that feeling.

## The three things the brief asked me to nail

1. **Models & textures (most important).** Hyperreal humans — Unreal 5 +
   MetaHuman base, custom skin/eye/hair shaders with subsurface scattering, pore
   micro-detail, wet eyes, sweat that grows over the night — **then run through a
   degraded camcorder/VHS filter** so the realism turns uncanny instead of
   sterile. The antagonist, the Night Technician, has a face engineered to be
   *almost* warm and therefore deeply wrong. Full spec in `01_ART_BIBLE.md`.

2. **Story (2nd).** A grounded human horror with a supernatural maybe: a clinic
   that harvests genuine terror from people too broke to ask questions. Ambiguous
   entity, complicit smiling villain, and a final reveal that recontextualizes
   the whole night. Full script in `02_STORY.md`.

3. **Gameplay (3rd).** Walk, look, interact, text, comply — and *sleep*. A
   breathe-to-sleep mechanic, an EEG monitor that is your only warning system, a
   sleep-paralysis sequence where the only thing you can move is your eyes, and a
   rule you will be desperate to break: **do not remove the sensors.** Full
   design in `03_GAMEPLAY_SYSTEMS.md`.

## The surprise (4th) — one sentence, no spoilers
The game is quietly building a clinical profile of **the real player** the entire
hour, and the antagonist's victory condition is *learning you* — culminating in a
finale assembled out of your own behavior and an end-card that is unsettlingly
correct about you. The full mechanism is in `04_THE_SURPRISE.md`.

## Tone words
Anesthetic. Fluorescent. Polite. Patient. Clean in a way that smells like it's
hiding something. *Kind eyes that don't blink enough.*

## Comparables
*Fears to Fathom* (charm, format, fuse) · *Visage* (dread of a space) ·
*MADiSON* (found-footage diegesis) · *Observation* / *SOMA* (the watcher theme) ·
the real-world unease of NHST sleep labs and medical sleep-paralysis research.

## Why a player finishes it and immediately tells a friend
Because the last screen knows what scared *them*, specifically — and the friend
won't believe it until they play it themselves.
