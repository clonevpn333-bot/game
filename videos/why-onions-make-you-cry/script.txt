Ever wondered why chopping an onion makes you cry?
It's actually a tiny chemical attack.
An onion is made of millions of tiny cells.
Inside, sulfur compounds and an enzyme are kept locked apart.
But when your knife slices through, the cells burst open, and the two collide.
Within seconds, they form an irritating gas.
It drifts up into your eyes, and triggers the nerves there.
Your brain thinks you're under attack, so it floods your eyes with tears to wash it away.
Want to stop it? Chill the onion first, and use a sharp knife.
Cold slows the reaction, and a sharp blade bursts fewer cells.
No more tears.
