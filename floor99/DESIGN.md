# FLOOR 99 — Design Bible

> "THE AUDIENCE WANTS A SHOW."

## 1. Pillars

1. **Fun in ten seconds.** No menus between you and the action. Floor 1 drops you into a fight
   within about 12 seconds of pressing PLAY, and it teaches by doing.
2. **The building is the enemy.** Every floor moves: presses, conveyors, pistons, rotating stages,
   floods, collapsing floors. Static arenas are a bug.
3. **The audience is a character.** Votes change the rules mid-fight, and the crowd reacts to your
   style. When ratings are high it helps you. When they are low it punishes you.
4. **Spectacle through play.** Big moments happen while you have control: you slide under the
   press yourself, and you are the one dodging the leaping shark.
5. **No bullet sponges.** Difficulty comes from telegraphed attacks, arena hazards and enemy
   combinations. Bosses are beaten by breaking weak points.

## 3. Story

### Cast
- **Contestant 99 (you):** a prisoner with no name on the manifest, only a number.
- **Mr. Halo:** the host, an AI with a TV-screen face, a halo and a bow tie. He is relentlessly
  upbeat at first. As you survive he gets irritated, then rattled, then unhinged. His dialogue
  pool is tiered by floor height.
- **Iris Vale:** a former arena engineer who designed half the tower's machinery. She talks to you
  over a hacked, encrypted channel. At first she helps only because she wants the show destroyed.

### Arc (beats marked ✔ are in the playable build)
- ✔ **Floor 1:** Halo's grand welcome. Iris interrupts the broadcast for the first time ("can you
  hear me? Don't answer"), and Halo covers for it. In the elevator she introduces herself: "I'm
  the reason that elevator still works."
- ✔ **Floor 4:** "Every robot you have destroyed was born right here." Iris admits she designed
  the presses. The Foreman "was here before the show. Before Halo." Iris: "Somebody built Halo to
  run this show. I intend to find out who."
- ✔ **Floor 12:** The viewer counter never changes when the cameras cut, so nobody is watching.
  Iris finds the contestant records: ninety-eight previous "winners", and none of them ever left
  the building. Halo glitches: "Every winner stays with the Halo family FOREVER!"
- **Floors 13–29:** Iris shows you the lower machine levels. The "audience" turns out to be a
  simulation, and the votes are generated by the tower itself to train something.
- **Floor 38 (You're Tiny):** In a shrunken kitchen you find Iris's old workstation. Her brother
  **Theo** was Contestant 98.
- **Floor 51 (Zero Gravity):** Theo's last log is recovered from a drifting capsule: "They
  don't kill the winners. They keep them."
- **Floor 76 (Labyrinth):** The labyrinth is a mind map. Halo is not a program; he is built
  from the uploaded minds of all ninety-eight winners, stitched together into a host. That is
  why he is coming apart.
- **Floor 90 (Final Broadcast):** You hijack the feed. For the first time the show goes out to
  the real world. The real audience finally sees what the tower is, and the building starts to
  malfunction.
- **Floor 99 (The Tower Itself):** The tower stands up. Elevators become limbs and floors become
  armour. You fight across the moving structure to the crown, where Halo's core sits inside the
  halo. When you break it, the ninety-eight voices go quiet. One of them, Theo's, says: "Tell
  Iris the lights were nice." The tower's walls open to a real sky. Iris walks out of the
  service elevator in person. The final card reads: **"THE AUDIENCE WANTS A SHOW." / "GIVE THEM
  THE TRUTH."**

## 4. Floor plan (99 floors, ten acts)

Every tenth floor brings a change in theme, mechanics or story.

| Act | Floors | Theme | New mechanic or twist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I — Pilot Episode | 1–9 | Studio, backstage, factory | Core movement and weapons, first votes, the Foreman (4) |
| II — Sweeps Week | 10–19 | Aquatic, cold storage, kitchens | Swimming, flooding, ice; Megamouth-9 (12); first procedural "challenge floors" |
| III — Syndication | 20–29 | Miniature city | Mech piloting (23); destruction at scale |
| IV — Cooking Segment | 30–39 | Giant domestic sets | Shrinking (38); appliance hazards |
| V — Prime Time | 40–49 | Jungle, holo-sims | Rule-swap floors (no guns, one-hit, mirror) |
| VI — Orbital Special | 50–59 | Space station | Zero-g traversal and combat (51); airlocks |
| VII — Reruns | 60–69 | Remixed earlier floors | The tower re-runs old sets corrupted; enemy remixes |
| VIII — The Maze | 70–79 | Transforming machinery | Labyrinth (76); rotating rooms; hunter AI |
| IX — Final Broadcast | 80–90 | The control levels | Hacking the feed (90); the audience turns |
| X — Series Finale | 91–99 | The tower awakens | Escalating chaos; floor 99 is the building itself as the boss |

### Signature floors not yet built
- **23 Mech City:** a 1:20-scale city. You get a 12 m combat mech with its own movement set
  (stomp, dash, shoulder rockets, grab-and-throw cars), destructible buildings built as fracture
  chunks, then a rival mech duel among the skyscrapers.
- **38 You're Tiny:** you are shrunk to 5 cm. A spoon is a bridge. Mutant insect swarms, a toaster
  that launches you, a dishwasher flood, and a fridge fortress. Audience votes switch appliances on.
- **51 Zero Gravity:** six-degrees-of-freedom movement with push-off, magnetic boots on hull
  plates, drifting debris used as cover, airlock decompression events, and votes that restore or
  reverse gravity in sections.
- **76 The Machine Labyrinth:** a grid of rotating room-cubes that re-path every 30 seconds.
  Stalkers hunt by sound. The exit route has to be read from the mechanism.
- **90 Final Broadcast:** a control-room assault where you hold broadcast nodes while the real
  audience "wakes up". Votes flip in your favour.
- **99 The Tower Itself:** a multi-stage climb up a moving titan. Elevators sweep as arms, floors
  tilt, and you grapple between limbs. Halo's core sits in the crown.

### Procedural floors
Non-signature floors use the shared kit (`src/floors/kit.js`): presses, hatches, movers, crowd
stands, screens, spotlights and elevators. They are assembled from room templates, a theme palette
and a rule card (for example "hydraulic", "low-light", "conveyor maze" or "flooded"). Each one gets
a vote pool and one unique set piece. They will be marked as procedural in Floor Select.

## 5. Systems architecture

- **Engine** (`src/engine`): WebGL renderer with ACES tone mapping, bloom and a broadcast grade
  pass (vignette, chromatic aberration, grain, underwater, glitch, letterbox, damage and heal
  tints). Custom physics uses oriented-box colliders, kinematic colliders that follow meshes
  (with delta transforms for platform carry), sphere characters, rigid bodies with buoyancy, and
  a uniform grid broadphase. FX uses two GPU point-particle systems, pooled lights, tracers,
  shockwave rings, fireballs, instanced decals, debris and shell pools. Textures and normal maps
  are generated procedurally.
- **Audio** (`src/audio`): synthesised SFX with stereo distance spatialisation and a reverb send.
  The arena crowd bed is driven by hype, with one-shots for cheers, boos, oohs and chants. The
  music is a step sequencer with layered instruments (drums, basses, pads, strings, brass, choir,
  e-piano, bells) and state-driven layer mixing.
- **Game** (`src/game`): `Game` is one floor session and owns combat resolution (hitscan,
  piercing, explosions, melee arcs and deflection, gravity grab and throw, chain yank). It also
  runs style scoring, hype, checkpoints and generator-based floor scripts. `Audience` runs votes
  with pluggable `VoteSource`s and an event runner. `Host` runs dialogue, portraits and voice.
  `NavGrid` provides flow-field pathfinding.
- **Floors** (`src/floors`): each floor exports `build(game)` (geometry, hazards, events, update)
  and `script(game, checkpoint)`, a coroutine of beats that can resume from any checkpoint.

## 6. Quality bar checklist (per floor)
- [x] At least one moving set piece the player interacts with
- [x] A scripted vote, plus auto-votes from the floor's pool
- [x] A traversal moment that is not combat
- [x] An enemy mix that forces weapon switching
- [x] Its own music track and colour palette
- [x] Checkpoints and instant retry
- [x] A story beat delivered during play
